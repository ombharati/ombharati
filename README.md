# Hi, I'm Om

I am a computer science student who grew up around computers. My fascination started early in childhood, mostly trying to figure out what was happening behind the screen when software ran. Over the years, that curiosity grew into a focus on systems, automation, and Linux, where mechanics are direct, predictable, and close to the hardware.

Outside of software, I spend time studying cinematography. Motion-picture photography sits at the boundary between mechanical precision and artistic intuition. Choosing a focal length, shaping shadows, and moving the camera with intent are technical actions, yet they dictate the emotional weight of a scene. It has taught me to pay attention to geometry, light, and quiet moments that usually pass unnoticed.

I also swim sometimes. There is a Daoist thought about water being yielding, yet capable of wearing down the hardest stone. Swimming reflects that truth. Thrashing against water accomplishes nothing; you only find momentum when you stop fighting resistance, align your body with the current, and move with it. It is one of the rare disciplines where calmness directly produces speed.

Riding a motorcycle offers a different sort of clarity. Everyday life tends to be buffered and sheltered, designed to insulate us from consequence. On the road, Sartre's observation that we are condemned to be free becomes immediate. You choose your line through every bend, carry full responsibility for every decision, and remain completely present with the asphalt. It is an unvarnished kind of autonomy.

I tend to gravitate toward things that give direct, honest feedback, whether that means debugging a process, swimming laps, framing a shot, or riding an empty stretch of road.
